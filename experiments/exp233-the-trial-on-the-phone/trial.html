What Lean proved, and what it refused

Quoted from exp232's proof/Cure.lean. This page checks none of it: it cannot run Lean.

  • M1 the board's 57-instruction kernel computes the model exactly, for any input, in 3081 instructions.
  • M2 a dose kills only cells at the edge of their kind — and tissue beside a tumour dies with it.
  • M3 untreated, any tumour fills the ring by generation 31: every control misses.
  • M4 the search's answer meets the endpoint for every enrolled patient, wherever the lump sits, and the tumour is gone for good — in the model.
  • M5 a schedule the search also accepted meets the same endpoint, and a lump of six comes back.

Refused by Lean: "the drug harms no tissue"; "the answer cures a lump of seven"; "whatever meets the endpoint is cured". The world's resistance is in none of these: the model has no such thing, so no theorem speaks of it.

How to read it

The board searched 1024 dosing schedules for one that meets the trial's endpoint — no tumour a scan can see at generation 24, half the tissue kept — and answered with the one with fewest doses. Then it draws patients from its random number generator, gives half the answer and half nothing, and lives each one twice: in the model the proof is about, and in a world where a dividing tumour cell is now and then resistant to the drug. The top panel is the trial as the board counts it. This page checks every line again: it runs the search itself, hashes the protocol itself, and replays each patient from the word it was drawn from.
