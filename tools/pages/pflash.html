Write firmware over PICOBOOT

No drive, no drag-and-drop. The board must be in BOOTSEL.

Choose a .uf2, then press Flash it. You will be asked to pick the board once — it appears as RP2350 Boot.




  Why this page and not the drive. A board with a partition table takes
  nothing from its BOOTSEL drive — the copy appears to succeed and the file is
  gone on the next mount. PICOBOOT does not go through the drive at all.




  If it fails. Nothing is written unless the pre-flight passes, and the
  board is not rebooted unless the read-back matches. A board with no bootable
  image drops into BOOTSEL by itself, so you can try again. If the board is
  dark instead — no drive, no ports — hold BOOTSEL while plugging it in.




  On a phone, the chooser may list the same board more than once — three
  entries, on the Pixel 9a this was verified on. Two of them failed
  open() with Access denied and one worked. There is nothing
  to read in the names: the live entry is the one that makes Android ask for
  USB permission. Picking a dead one costs nothing, because the page has not
  sent a single command by then.
